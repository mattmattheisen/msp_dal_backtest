# Fleet-constraint gate results

## Question
Did the post-COVID airline capacity signal become powerful because airlines able to grow ASM also had privileged access to scarce aircraft / physical fleet capacity?

## Data
BTS Schedule B-43 annual aircraft inventory, 2010-2025, six-carrier universe (AA, AS, B6, DL, UA, WN), operating-status Y only. Metrics: operating aircraft count, operating seats, YoY fleet growth, YoY operating-seat growth, average fleet age, and share of aircraft <=5 years old. Joined to BTS T1 scheduled-service ASM history.

## Primary structural check
If the proposed mechanism is correct, ASM growth should have become more tightly linked to physical fleet/seat growth in the post-COVID period than pre-COVID.

Observed pooled carrier-year correlations:

| Period | n carrier-years | Corr(ASM YoY, aircraft-count YoY) | Corr(ASM YoY, operating-seat YoY) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2014-2019 | 36 | 0.813 | 0.810 |
| 2022-2025 | 24 | 0.476 | 0.487 |

The direction is opposite the preregistered mechanism. In the modern period, ASM growth is materially *less* tied to annual physical fleet growth than it was pre-COVID.

## Interpretation
This falsifies the simple story that the modern equity signal exists because fast ASM growers uniquely possess scarce aircraft/delivery capacity. Fleet availability may still matter economically, but B-43 does not support it as the primary explanation for the regime break.

The result points instead toward utilization/network deployment or forward-looking management decisions: modern ASM changes can be generated by aircraft utilization, gauge, route/network scheduling, and redeployment without proportional changes in year-end fleet count.

## Data-quality note
One anomalous 2023 UA average-age calculation was observed due to manufacture-year values requiring additional cleaning. It does not affect the primary fleet-count or operating-seat-growth correlations above, but age-based secondary tests should not be interpreted until manufacture-year validation is completed.

## Research discipline
Do not add fleet variables to the trading rule based on this gate. The preregistered structural hypothesis failed its primary implication. Next test should distinguish *utilization/deployment growth* from *physical fleet growth*, ideally ASM per operating aircraft / ASM per operating seat, using only information available at the signal date.
